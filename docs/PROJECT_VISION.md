# Project vision

## Problem
Ghanaian organizations need accessible, credible, locally relevant cyber threat intelligence to inform detection, investigation, defensive priorities, and management decisions. Global reporting is useful but must not be mistaken for evidence of Ghana-specific targeting.

## Goal
Develop a continuously operating, low-cost, AI-assisted CTI pipeline with a publicly accessible intelligence publication channel. Serve all Ghanaian organizations connected to the internet, not solely CIIS. Some outputs may require restricted distribution under TLP 2.0.

## Audiences and products
- Threat hunters and SOC teams: tactical IOCs, techniques, detection/hunting guidance, and evidence limitations.
- Incident responders and security leads: operational campaign context, affected technologies/sectors, mitigations.
- Leadership and risk teams: evidence-based strategic implications and recommended decisions.
- Public readers: approved TLP:CLEAR articles and advisories.

## Scope
Start with tactical and operational CTI. Build records that can later support quarterly and annual Ghana-centric threat reports, with strategic analysis based on aggregated verified evidence.

## Not in scope
- A DNS-only or certificate-transparency-only project.
- A Microsoft-outage-specific project: the November 2026 Microsoft phishing report is a reference use case for quality and structure.
- Autonomous threat-actor attribution or publication without analyst approval.
- Claiming a threat targeted Ghana merely because it could affect Ghana.

## Success criteria
Reliable collection; traceable source evidence; useful Ghana relevance ranking; analyst review queue; verified published intelligence; low operational costs; extensible source adapters; repeatable annual reporting from stored validated findings.
